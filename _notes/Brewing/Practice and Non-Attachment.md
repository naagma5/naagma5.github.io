Alrighty so this morning I ventured into the Twelfth Sutra: Abhyāsavairāgyābhām tannirodhah.
Desichakar sweetly and simply translates it as "The mind can reach the state of Yoga through practice and detachment" (Heart of Yoga, 153). His entire section on it is about 3 lines. 

With the Heart of Yoga book alone, I find it ironic how simple this sutra is written. However, when you really think about, this is a fundamental sutra carrying so much power.

Practice and non-attachment. Practice and non-attachment.

A side note here. There’s so many definitions of [[yoga]] floating around in my brain now. *Quieting the fluctuations of the mind.* Is it a *mind state*? Is it *the practice itself*? Is it *all the eight limbs*? Future contemplation to be had. 

Alright stay one-pointed! ;) 

Practice and non-attachment. That’s IT. I absolutely love that. It really makes sense for me. 

In The Secret of the Yoga Sutra, Pandit Rajmani Tigunait goes deeper into his translation and interpretation of the Sutra (pg 37-41) and breaks down abhyāsa and vairāgyā. 

#### Abhyāsa
Consider the ways in which we become who we are. I believe the psychology world has finally stopped arguing over whether it's nature vs. nurture and agrees there is both factors when it comes to becoming who we are. So many thoughts, feelings, experiences over our lifetime influence us. Habits and beliefs are born out of conscious or unconscious conditioning and reinforcement. As creatures of comfort, even if a certain thought/habit/action is "bad" for us, we will continue on that track. 

I'll share an embarrassing personal story. In my marriage, I can be very bossy and act as the project manager for the household (see [[mental load]]). One example, is trash day. Monday is trash day for us and every Monday morning, my brain continuously pushes out thoughts around "It's trash day", "It's also recycle", "Trash needs to be taken out", "It's 6pm. Do you know where your trash is?". The thing is... I don't own trash maintainance in my household. My husband does. I need to fully delete trash duty as a samkara. Tell me why this is SO hard to do. I'm plagued with sweats, ALL the fluctuations. Now I'm thinking about, will he or won't he remember? What happens if he does (ah, calm)? BUT WHAT HAPPENS IF HE DOESN'T? I should just go do it. Right? NO! HE OWNS TRASH. 

Tl;dr my personality has become trash czar. I need to recalibrate my brain to "delete" this samskara (impression) that has now become a vasana (a strong samskara coloring the mind). 

Here comes the practice, abhyāsā. I think we'll get more into this in future sutras but it's not about going this thought will now be deleted from my mind, goodbye trash duty. It's more about creating space, making room for discernment, practicing noticing, observation, becoming aware so that you can get to a point where you can choose. I think. 

You get to a point where you can choose differently. This parallels all the rage about [[neuroplasticity]]. 

(By the way. My husband did remember and he did it in his own way. Sigh. Win for him. Take that trash czar.) 

#### Vairāgyā
Patanjali doesn't just stop at practice, though. He recognizes the importance of taking action along with the effort and disclicine required to choose differently and lighten the samskaras. However, he recognizes that this may become an obsession and bring about anxiety if we aren't careful. We may stress ourselves out by trying too hard at achieving a state of peace. So vairāgyā is so important as a way of practicing non-attachment. "This is where vairāgyā assists abhyāsā, allowing us to make a deep commitment to our practice of abhyāsā while keeping us free from anxiety about its fruit" (Tigunait, 41). 

Practice, be effortful in your actions, TRY YOUR BEST. Then when you've done all you've can, let it go. 

I can feel my whole body relaxing. As someone who identifies as a high-achiever particularly in high school, I've carried a sense of anxiety and burnt out many times. What if this happens or what if I don't get the job after trying SO hard? 

What if I don't need to worry about what happens and attach myself to the results? As a wise man once said, "Don't worry about the scoreboard, you can do anything that you work for."
